Boring Phone — Privacy Policy

What Boring Phone does with your data

Last updated 18 September 2026

The short version: Boring Phone doesn't collect, transmit, or share any data at all. Everything below explains exactly why, in the same amount of detail a curious user deserves.

01

No accounts, no servers

Boring Phone has no sign-in, no user accounts, and no backend server it talks to. The app never makes a network request during normal use. There is nothing to log in to and nothing to leak.

02

What's stored, and where

Two things live on your device only, in standard iOS app storage (UserDefaults), and never leave it:

  • Whether the app-blocking shield is currently on or off.
  • Which apps you've chosen to stay reachable, stored as opaque tokens issued by Apple's Screen Time API — not as app names, bundle identifiers, or anything Boring Phone itself can read back as plain text.

Deleting the app deletes this data. There is no cloud backup, sync, or copy anywhere else.

03

Screen Time / Family Controls

Boring Phone uses Apple's FamilyControls and ManagedSettings frameworks to shield apps you haven't allow-listed. This permission is requested directly through Apple's own system dialog, is enforced entirely by iOS, and Boring Phone never receives a list of the apps installed on your phone — only the picker's own privacy-preserving selection tokens, which the app itself cannot decode into identities.

04

NFC tags and Shortcuts

The NFC tag that locks and unlocks your phone is registered entirely inside Apple's own Shortcuts app, using Apple's own NFC automation feature. Boring Phone never reads, stores, or has access to your tag's identifier — Shortcuts recognizes the tag and calls one of Boring Phone's on/off actions, that's the entire interaction.

05

No analytics, no third parties

No analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier, no third-party library of any kind is included in this app. Nothing is sold, shared, or monetized — there's nothing to monetize, since nothing is collected.

06

Children's privacy

Boring Phone is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

07

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes — for instance, if a future version adds a feature that touches data in a new way — the date at the top of this page will change, and the new version will describe exactly what changed and why.

Questions about this policy, or about the app generally? See the Support page for how to reach the developer.
